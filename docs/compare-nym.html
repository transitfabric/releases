Fabric and Nym: different layers, complementary networks

Status of this page: Transit Fabric is live on Bittensor testnet, with mainnet planned for 1 January 2027. Facts about Nym come from Nym's own documentation, read on 2026-11-21 (sources at the end). Nothing on this page is an earnings or price claim, and nothing has been discussed with Nym.

The short version

Nym and Transit Fabric both pay independent operators to carry other people's traffic, and both measure that work before paying for it. They answer different questions:

  • Nym asks: how do packets cross the internet without anyone being able to tell who is talking to whom? It is a privacy network. It deliberately adds delay and cover traffic, because that is what defeats traffic analysis.
  • Fabric asks: how do packets cross the internet on the best measured path, over capacity that independent operators really have? It is a transit and routing network. It removes delay wherever measurement shows a faster path exists.

Nym is a privacy layer that runs on top of the internet. Fabric is a transit layer underneath applications, closer to a backbone. They are not competitors. A privacy network like Nym is the kind of network that could carry its own traffic between its nodes over Fabric.

Decentralized VPN networks such as Mysterium and Sentinel are also sometimes compared with Fabric. They differ from it in the same way: they are exit and VPN networks, which send a user's traffic out to the internet, while Fabric is transit between nodes.

Why the difference in layers matters

Privacy and speed pull in opposite directions. A mixnet's protection comes from mixing: delays, reordering and cover traffic, so timing reveals nothing. A transit fabric's value comes from the opposite: the shortest measured path and real capacity. One network can't maximise both, which is why they sit at different layers.

Fabric doesn't look inside traffic, and doesn't hide who is talking either. Fabric links are encrypted (WireGuard), and every node has a key-derived address. But Fabric is built to route fast, not to stop an observer linking flows by timing. Anyone who needs that protection needs a privacy layer such as Nym on top.

Nym doesn't choose the internet path between its nodes. A Nym packet's hops between gateways and mix nodes cross the ordinary internet, on whatever paths the operators' upstream providers pick. That is the layer Fabric works on.

How Nym could use Fabric

This is a possibility, not an arrangement. Nothing has been discussed with Nym.

  • Carrying the hops between Nym nodes. A Nym operator running a Fabric node next to a gateway or mix node could send its node-to-node traffic over Fabric's measured paths and capacity, not over the default internet route. That is Fabric's launch product: transit between sites, with VPN providers named as a first market.
  • Exits stay Nym's. Fabric transit nodes never egress to the internet, so Fabric would carry Nym's internal hops and leave its exit gateways as they are.
  • Privacy is Nym's call. Fabric would see only Sphinx-encrypted packets between Nym nodes, and it adds no new view of who is talking to whom inside the mixnet. But Fabric nodes would see the outer flows between Nym nodes, as any internet transit provider does today. Whether that is acceptable belongs in Nym's own threat model.
  • How it would be bought. Through a reseller, as described in the FAQ, with prices quoted in US dollars.

What the two have in common

  • Independent operators are paid for work the network measures, not for claims.
  • Both started on network-funded rewards and aim to be paid by real usage.
  • Both put performance ahead of size: a node that performs poorly earns little, however much it has behind it.
